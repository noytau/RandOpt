ExDark — Honest Train/Test Protocol: RandOpt vs Full Fine-Tuning
Generated August 5, 2026  |  W&B project: noyhassid-tau/randopt

1. What Was Tested

ExDark (Exclusively Dark Image Dataset) is a 12-class low-light/dark image classification benchmark — the first of several new datasets chosen specifically because each ships its own official train/val/test split. That matters: the project's earlier ImageNet-C distribution-shift suite scored RandOpt's perturbations on clean ImageNet-train and evaluated on a different dataset/corruption, conflating domain shift with train/test methodology. This series fixes that — scoring and evaluation both happen inside one dataset's own splits.

🌙 Dataset

ExDark, 12 classes (Bicycle, Boat, Bottle, Bus, Car, Cat, Chair, Cup, Dog, Motorbike, People, Table)
Official split: train 3000 / val 1800 / test 2563 (imageclasslist.txt col 5)

🧠 Model

DINOv2 ViT-g/14 backbone (1.1B params) + a freshly-fit 12-way linear head (no pretrained ExDark head exists to download, unlike ImageNet-1k)

Data-leak caught and fixed mid-series The first RandOpt/FT runs scored perturbations on a sample of the same "train" images the linear head was fit on — train_reward hit 0.999 (near-perfect, i.e. memorization), while the honest test_accuracy was only ~85%. Fixed by carving a stratified 600-image train_holdout (50/class) out of the official train split, disjoint from the ~2400 images the head is fit on, and re-fitting the head on the reduced set. All results below use the corrected, leak-free holdout.

2. Protocol

Phase 0 — Fit the head: Linear probe (backbone frozen), AdamW lr=1e-3, 10 epochs, on the ~2400-image reduced train split. Converges to 99.8% train accuracy — this becomes the "center" both RandOpt and FT start from, playing the same role Meta's released ImageNet-1k head plays elsewhere in this project.
Phase 1 — Base eval: The unmodified (backbone + fitted head) model, evaluated on train_holdout (scoring set) and test (eval set).
Phase 2a — RandOpt: N Gaussian perturbations (σ ∈ {2, 5, 10}×10⁻⁴) over the entire model (backbone + head), each scored by accuracy on the 600-image train_holdout; top-K=50 selected by score; majority-vote ensemble on the full 2563-image test split.
Phase 2b — Full FT: Same starting center, same train_holdout data budget (600 images), same full-model scope — but AdamW gradient descent (5 epochs) instead of perturb-and-select. The gradient-based counterpart to RandOpt, budget-matched for a fair comparison.
Phase 3 — Catastrophic-forgetting check: Take the exact backbone each method produced, swap the ExDark head back out for Meta's original frozen ImageNet-1k head (unperturbed, unmodified), and evaluate on 6 ImageNet-family targets. Noise is generated per-parameter from (seed, that parameter's shape) — identical regardless of which head is attached — so RandOpt's backbone-only replay reproduces the exact same weights its ExDark ensemble members had.

5. Catastrophic-Forgetting Check — 6 ImageNet-Family Datasets

Both the RandOpt ensemble and the FT'd model adapt the entire backbone to ExDark. Does that damage the backbone's original ImageNet-1k ability? 6. Key Findings

  • The train/test leak mattered. Scoring on data the head had memorized inflated train_reward to 0.999 and made the setup look healthier than it was — always sanity-check train reward against test accuracy for exactly this gap.
  • For ExDark specifically, full FT beats RandOpt — +3.04pp vs +0.04pp, the opposite of the earlier ImageNet-C series where small-budget FT damaged accuracy and RandOpt was the safer choice. Not a universal rule; dataset-dependent.
  • RandOpt's population size doesn't matter here — N=2000 and N=3000 give identical ensemble accuracy (84.74%). The needle isn't in a bigger haystack.
  • FT's learning rate is a cliff, not a slope — 1e-5 works, 3e-5 already overfits into a net loss, 1e-4 diverges outright. Train-set memorization (reward=1.0) does not imply the LR was reasonable.
  • Forgetting, when it happens, is concentrated, not diffuse — FT's ImageNet-family damage is almost entirely on imagenet_es (−8.1pp); the other 5 targets are within ±1.6pp.

Chaining doesn't close the gap with FT

The multi-step feature was built on the hypothesis that a single perturbation step wasn't moving DINOv3 far enough from its pretrained center to reach an informative region — chaining P steps at the same σ was meant to reach further without needing a riskier, larger σ. This P=3 run is just as flat as the original P=1 result (+0.0 vs −0.1pp, both within noise), while even the mildest FT setting still finds a small but real +0.1 to +0.6pp gain. Gradient descent keeps finding small genuine improvements that pure random search — chained or not — doesn't, at this budget.

One data point, one dataset This run covered imagenet_c only, at N=2000, P=3. It doesn't rule out P=5 or a different dataset telling a different story — it just doesn't support the chaining hypothesis yet. With the RAM fix confirmed, the full P=2→3→5, all-6-dataset sweep can now run for a complete picture.

ExDark on DINOv3 — RandOpt vs Full FT

ExDark so far had only run on DINOv2 (sections 1–6 above). Same 12-class ExDark head-fitting protocol, ported to DINOv3, then run as two experiments: (1) adapt on clean ImageNet (both FT and RandOpt), check transfer to ExDark; (2) adapt on ExDark's own split (both methods), check transfer to the 6 ImageNet-family datasets. Both methods: FT lr=1e-5/all-layers, RandOpt N=2000/K=50.

Fresh ExDark head fit for DINOv3 DINOv2 and DINOv3 have different embedding dimensions, so the DINOv2 series' head can't be reused — a new one was fit the same way (backbone frozen, AdamW lr=1e-3, 10 epochs, on the same 2400-image reduced train split): train_acc=99.71%, held-out test_acc=85.21% — a healthy train/test gap for a linear probe on strong frozen features, not memorization, and close to the DINOv2 head's own 99.8%/84.71%.

DINOv3's FT forgetting footprint is far milder than DINOv2's

The DINOv2 ExDark series (section 5 above) found FT damaged imagenet_es by −8.1pp — a real, concentrated cost for its +3.04pp ExDark gain. DINOv3's equivalent FT run gains less on ExDark (+0.47pp) but also damages far less: max deviation across all 6 ImageNet-family datasets is just ±0.25pp, nothing close to a concentrated casualty like DINOv2's imagenet_es hit.
